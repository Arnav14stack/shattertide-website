FIELD GUIDE / 01

Start here

The power of choice.

What is SHATTERTIDE?

A planned PC combat and survival game on a mountainous island surrounded by a dangerous ocean. Choose a Technique, sword configuration, ranged weapon and Aura. Fight, explore, cooperate, rescue someone, build a crew or hunt something enormous. The world creates circumstances; you decide what to do about them.

Link to entry ↗
Skill and access

Combat advantages come from timing, execution, movement, observation, positioning and knowledge. The design has no gameplay currency or repetitive resource grind. Cosmetics give no combat advantage. New players and veterans have access to the same fundamental combat possibilities. Gameplay additions from events are intended to remain available afterward.

Link to entry ↗
How a match unfolds

A protected offshore sloop brings players toward the island, choosing among different coastal approaches. New matches begin with 4 minutes 30 seconds without player-versus-player damage. You can already move, explore and prepare; when the grace period ends, combat begins wherever you are. Players may join ongoing matches until the active population falls below 20. The working match size is roughly 50 players.

Link to entry ↗
Survival and victory

The last player or team remaining wins. There is no overall match timer. The Tide eventually stops rising, so surviving players can continue exploring or fighting. Victory brings an extraction ship, a departure and one final disaster: the ship is destroyed, but your first-place recognition remains. Death fades the world and audio toward black before the title DEATH OF THE LEGEND and a return to the lobby.

Link to entry ↗
Read the world

Real contact matters. A Kraken grab restrains; its hooks injure. A serpent circling you is different from a serpent constricting you. A ship ram depends on the physical impact. Observe the world, learn patterns and judge risks alongside practicing your combat execution.

FIELD GUIDE / 02

Island & Tide

One island. A changing battlefield.

The handcrafted island

The same island returns each match: a jagged emerald and near-black landmass, a mountainous outer ring and a densely forested interior. Connected ridges, saddles, valleys and passes create routes inward. Arrival locations and encounter territories can vary while the major geography stays familiar.

Link to entry ↗
Rising Tide

Low routes flood first. Rivers widen, valleys fill and low mountain saddles disappear, severing walking routes. Water does not automatically deal damage; deeper water opens new territory to ocean creatures. The planned cadence is a rise at the start of each new in-game day. Exact height increments remain provisional.

Link to entry ↗
Max Tide and the archipelago

The ocean has a finite maximum. Selected peaks, high ridges and mountain forests remain above water, turning the island into an archipelago. Former villages, valleys and connecting ridges remain underwater. There is still dry terrain for exploration and combat after Max Tide.

Link to entry ↗
Mount Sentinel and Outer Ring Peak

Mount Sentinel is colder, rockier and harsher, with tree growth concentrated lower on its upper slopes and increasingly exposed rock toward its summit. Outer Ring Peak stays greener and grassier. Silhouettes and vegetation help you recognize the mountains. Summits offer views of the changing island.

Link to entry ↗
The evacuated village

A small, largely intact settlement lies in a mountain basin beside the meeting of the two largest rivers. Its residents left after warnings of catastrophic flooding from a melting ice-covered landmass. There are no villagers or playable building interiors. Streets and exterior spaces are accessible until the rising water submerges them.

Link to entry ↗
Tiber Rapids

Two major rivers converge beside the village, then continue as one river toward the ocean. Their rocky channel produces fast water, eddies and standing waves. Currents influence players, boats and floating objects. A boat that fits can travel downstream, with steering, rocks and collisions supplying the danger. Rising sea levels widen and eventually drown the channels.

Link to entry ↗
Coasts and navigation

Cliffs, coves, shelves, sea stacks and submerged ledges make route knowledge useful. Navigable channels still exist. Flooding creates new shorelines across existing rock and vegetation rather than producing a perfect beach at every new waterline.

Link to entry ↗
Day and night

The planned cycle is five minutes of day followed by five minutes of night. Most creatures follow their own encounter conditions rather than a universal nighttime rule. The Abyssal Leviathan is the exception: it is active during official Night and retreats deeper when daylight returns.

FIELD GUIDE / 03

Loadouts & combat

Four choices. Many interactions.

Your four selections

Technique: your main supernatural combat system. Sword / SK: your sword choice and multi-sword configuration. Ranged: your firearm or other ranged weapon. Aura: additional ranged functions or visual modifiers. The loadout menu supports browsing and searching before you join a match.

Link to entry ↗
Swords, blocking and SK

Sword combat uses directional strikes, blocking and clashes. Effects that need blade contact do not pass through a successful block. SK configurations range from 1SK to 6SK. SK count determines the overall Domain activation choreography, while restrictions on an individual sword affect that sword’s part of the animation.

Link to entry ↗
Domains in a moving world

When a Domain is established aboard a moving vessel, the vessel momentarily stops. A mobile Domain can then travel with the ship; an anchored Domain keeps it stopped for its active duration. Different Domains can have different mobility rules. Domain activation requires both hands available.

Link to entry ↗
Large creatures and local effects

Domains affect the portions of a large creature actually caught inside. Freezing one section does not freeze every untouched part. The creature remains physically connected, so a frozen middle still restricts how the rest can move.

Link to entry ↗
The every-17-hits bonus

The global Black Flash mechanic counts melee hits only. Bullets, shotgun pellets and other ranged hits do not build that counter. Black Flash Aura is the separate way to gain ranged Black Flash functionality.

Link to entry ↗
Blacksmiths and broken weapons

A snapped sword remains your weapon: it is not permanently lost and does not automatically regrow. Bring it to a Blacksmith for a free repair and wait for the work to finish. Repair takes time, making route choice and safety matter. Blacksmiths are usually near Volcanic Spring entrances. Lingering near a Blacksmith without repairing eventually affects Will; passing through or actually repairing is allowed.

FIELD GUIDE / 04

Techniques

Abilities and the rules that make them distinct.

Articlame

Temperature and material manipulation, including the materials or states named AGVN, NERO and AVN, and plasma/liquid interactions. The supplied overview names these elements without providing a complete move-by-move reference.

Link to entry ↗
VOID — Lightspeed

VOID manipulates effective distance. Electrified Blue, Electrified Red and Supernova-type interactions accompany its Domain, Lightspeed. Zero Distance compresses traversal; Redshift stretches the distance approaching attacks must cover. Lightbreak creates a network of points for rapid travel and attacks. The finishing strike compresses a straight path almost to zero. VOID changes distance rather than time, literal teleportation or the physical size of props. Projectile range follows the effective distance the shot must travel.

Link to entry ↗
Disidente

An adaptation and counter Technique with four established adaptations. Earning an adaptation requires roughly one to two minutes of continuous observation of the relevant Technique. An opponent who keeps switching Techniques can deny that study. Your other weapons, Aura and existing adaptations remain usable while you observe.

Link to entry ↗
Electricity — Plasma

Includes Static, Divine Lightning, electrical movement and weapon electricity. Its Plasma Domain escalates electrical events and ends in an arc-flash-style collapse with electrical discharge, intense heat and blast effects. Plasma lasts longer than a normal Domain. If Blade of Styx destroys it early, its activity fades over roughly 30 seconds without the final explosion; a normal Domain destroyed by Styx fades over roughly three seconds.

Link to entry ↗
Rubber

Elastic abilities include Catapult, Rubber Hand and transformations named Rock/Morphastion, Meat and God. Rubber is immune to direct electrical effects. That immunity does not protect against every component of Plasma’s final collapse: its heat and blast can still hurt. Rubber Rock can survive Astéri.

Link to entry ↗
Blood

Blood manipulation combines healing, Heat Repulsion, frozen-blood weaponry and a Healing Domain.

Link to entry ↗
Polarised Light

Manipulates light and polarization through abilities including Cross-Light, Null Angle, Prism Break and Polarised Edge, with associated Domain concepts. Its identity is distinct from VOID’s distance control and Clock Sword’s time manipulation.

Link to entry ↗
Aquilo

Wind manipulation used to push, slash and move. Includes Tailwind/Gale-style movement, pressure and vacuum attacks, weapon integration and wind Domain concepts.

Link to entry ↗
Ice — Glacial Epoch

Ice Shard fires a group of ice projectiles. Self Engagement encloses the user and opponent inside a round ice enclosure. Ice Barrier provides a temporary, breakable defense. Glacial Epoch has two manually selected forms: Antarctic Deep Freeze transforms the existing region into a frozen version of itself, preserving its geography and freezing affected water; Total Freeze briefly freezes and lightly damages what it catches. Local freezing lasts roughly three seconds. Frozen water can trap ships. The transformed environment itself does not automatically damage fighters.

Link to entry ↗
Provisional swamp Technique

A frog-and-swamp concept includes a tongue grab, banjo club, Lily Pad blindness and Swamp of Serenity. Its final public identity is not established. This entry describes a provisional concept rather than a finalized named Technique.

FIELD GUIDE / 08

Ocean creatures

Watch what they do, and what actually touches you.

Kraken

Tentacles restrain and drag. The hooks are the damaging structures, so a grab alone is not the same as being injured. Tentacles can be severed, and strikes affect the part you actually hit.

Link to entry ↗
Megalodon

An enormous animal that patrols deep water, investigates disturbance and may circle before committing. Blood and injury can increase detection. Its high-speed charge needs actual jaw contact to bite; a missed charge forces it to turn. Fin and tail injuries can affect movement. It can breach where depth and space allow. Skin contact causes tiny abrasions rather than bite-level damage.

Link to entry ↗
Sea Dragon / Serpent

A smooth, continuous eel/dragon-like creature with armored scales and weaker seams. Its signature Coil starts with wide circles, tightens around the player and ends in constriction. Being surrounded or touching its body does not automatically cause crushing damage. It can make fast underwater passes and breach or body-slam.

Link to entry ↗
Charybdis

Occupies an unmarked territory that changes between matches. A subtle, unusually dark-blue clarity is a warning. When four or more players enter, enormous teeth rise around the perimeter and the water begins rotating inward. The center is extremely dangerous, and individual teeth are physical hazards. Will is affected when the current meaningfully catches and drags you inward, rather than draining continuously throughout the encounter.

Link to entry ↗
Scylla

Occupies a territory that changes each match. Two or more players trigger the appearance of roughly nine to twelve dragon-like heads on long necks. Heads attack semi-independently. Bites need actual jaw/teeth contact, and headbutts depend on the collision. Mere contact with a neck is not automatically a major damaging attack. An additional Will-related effect remains provisional.

Link to entry ↗
Abyssal Leviathan

An immense eel/anaconda-like creature with a bioluminescent spine, active only during official Night. Its primary target is your boat. It coils to stress the hull, generates waves to capsize vessels and rams them. Players can fight back and kill it. After destroying a ship, it generally leaves the people in the water. At dawn it disengages and retreats deeper. After Max Tide, rare migrations can carry it through the newly flooded interior.

Link to entry ↗
Abyssal Angler

A darkness-dependent predator inspired by gulper-eel proportions. It can appear at extreme daytime depths as well as at night. False Light lures prey toward a body hidden in the darkness and can slightly affect Will. Its jaw unfolds dramatically to engulf prey. It may abandon you for easier food, extinguish its lure or vanish into dark water. The lure can be severed without killing the creature. Being swallowed offers a short chance to fight your way out before the encounter becomes lethal.

FIELD GUIDE / 09

Wildlife

Not every animal wants a boss fight.

Ordinary fish

Background wildlife swims, eats, flees, reproduces and becomes prey. These are living parts of the ocean rather than collectible rarity tiers.

Link to entry ↗
The happy Oarfish

A huge Oarfish-inspired animal with a red crest, long body and exceptionally happy-looking face. It eats fish, investigates players and wanders near ships without becoming hostile. Its physical size can still cause collisions. Being nearby slightly improves Will regeneration, with a smaller Health-regeneration benefit. It keeps wandering rather than following injured players as a healer.

Link to entry ↗
Land animals

Rabbits and deer flee danger and changing habitat. Goats traverse mountainous terrain. Bears mainly eat berries and are normally peaceful. Animals moving away from flooded lowlands or disturbances can help observant players notice changes. The bear does not share the Oarfish’s regeneration perk.

FIELD GUIDE / 10

Ships & travel

Find a crew. Choose a direction.

Building and sailing ships

Floating docks let players create ships without spending currency or grinding resources. A vessel requires enough people to stand in its marked crew zone. Different sizes support different groups. Sail for transport, combat, exploration or monster hunting; navigation, repair and fighting roles emerge from what your crew chooses to do.

Link to entry ↗
Hull damage and wreckage

Damage is localized. Impacts, weapon strikes and creature attacks can break hull sections, open holes, affect buoyancy and capsize or destroy a vessel. Floating planks, hull pieces and sail fragments can remain as temporary aftermath, drifting with currents or washing toward shore and docks.

Link to entry ↗
Floating Docks

Docks rise with the Tide. At Max Tide their irregular platforms and connectors remain useful surface routes and fighting spaces alongside surviving peaks.

Link to entry ↗
Volcanic Spring tunnels

Fast water carries you continuously through underground tunnels in volcanic rock and ejects you elsewhere on the island. The destination is randomized, making these relocation routes rather than precision fast travel. Blacksmiths are generally found near an entrance.

Link to entry ↗
Beyond the horizon

Sail continuously away from the island and it can eventually appear ahead again, as though you have circumnavigated the world. The open sea has no countdown forcing you to turn around.

Link to entry ↗
The deepest dive

At extreme depth, continued downward travel can eventually bring you toward brighter water and the surface again. There is no arbitrary maximum-depth warning. The darkness on that journey can still contain an Abyssal Angler; the journey does not grant protection.

FIELD GUIDE / 11

Will, movement & player rules

Stay composed. Help someone. Take a break.

Health and Will

Health and Will appear as compact percentages. Will measures composure under specific pressures; it is not stamina. Normal fighting does not drain it. Explicit interactions, such as the Living Sword, the Angler’s False Light or being caught by Charybdis, can affect it. Low Will changes perception and presentation rather than imposing arbitrary damage penalties, random misses or broken controls. You can still win through skill at zero Will.

Link to entry ↗
Helping another player

E is the contextual interaction key. At a valid physical grab point it can help pull someone from a ledge or water, support an impaired player or let several players pull together. Press E again to release. Reach and strength remain physical limits. Occupying your hands with a grab prevents Domain activation.

Link to entry ↗
Ledges and falling

There is no generic fall-damage HP penalty. A physically possible ledge catch happens automatically and lasts roughly ten seconds, giving you a chance to recover or receive help. If the grip expires, you fall. The danger comes from the destination: water, isolation, creatures or terrain.

Link to entry ↗
Safe pause

The pause menu opens immediately. When no committed danger remains unresolved, your character can fade out of normal combat. Attacks, projectiles, bites, grabs and other dangers already in motion still resolve. Safely pausing preserves Health, Will, weapons, cooldowns and status rather than restoring them. Returning places you at a random dry location, avoiding immediate threats where possible.

Link to entry ↗
Final 15 pause rule

At 15 players remaining, indefinite pausing ends. A player already paused returns to the lobby. You can still leave for real-life reasons, but cannot reserve an invisible place in the endgame.

Link to entry ↗
Friends and communication

Playing with friends does not require an additional social fee. Communication uses text built from a broad approved vocabulary, with suggestions for words outside it. There is no unrestricted voice chat. The vocabulary supports useful directions, actions, questions and ordinary conversation.

Link to entry ↗
Camera, avatar and menus

Switch between first and third person without changing your stats. Base avatar appearances include black, white and blue, with hats, glasses, jackets, shirts and pants. Cosmetics provide no combat advantage. The dark lobby focuses on Play and Customize Avatar, with Settings readily available. The pause menu includes Resume, Friends, Settings and Leave Match.

Link to entry ↗
Sound and information

Music follows the state of the match, with deep-water and active-encounter changes below the priority of Final 10, Final 5 and victory. Simply being near a creature does not trigger warning music. Ship cracking, impacts and environmental sounds remain useful cues. Master, Music and SFX have separate volume controls.
